Thariq argues that with Claude Fable 5, output quality is bottlenecked by your ability to clarify unknowns, the gap between the map (prompts, skills, context) and the territory (codebase, real constraints). He catalogs patterns for surfacing unknowns before, during and after implementation, often via HTML artifacts.

## Key Takeaways
- Unknowns are the gap between what you give Claude and what the work needs; Claude guesses when it hits one
- Too-specific instructions block good pivots; too-vague ones invite generic assumptions
- Tell Claude your starting point and experience, and treat it as a thought partner
- Pre-implementation "blind spot pass": ask Claude to surface unknown unknowns and explain them
- Discovery is iterative: before, during and after implementation
