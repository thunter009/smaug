Essay arguing that VCs, after inflating private companies to trillions, are engineering ways to sell into retail and passive retirement capital.
Companies now stay private until public markets are only where value is realized, not created, which breaks the American bargain that stock ownership replaces a welfare state.

## Key Takeaways

- 401k/index-fund bargain assumed public markets capture company growth; trillion-dollar private companies move that growth to pre-IPO cap tables (Figma -50% and Klarna -90% from private marks).
- "Democratization" vehicles like USVC distribute insiders' existing positions to retail rather than give real access.
- Crypto precedent: locked foundation tokens wrapped in equity vehicles so TradFi and retail buyers absorbed supply.
- Proposed Nasdaq rule gives low-float new listings index weights calibrated at 5x float; a SpaceX-scale IPO could force passive funds to buy hundreds of billions right as lock-ups expire.
- Author links the widening early/late class gap to public anger at tech (attacks on Waymos, data center protests).
